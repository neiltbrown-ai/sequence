<CbSection id="thesis">

<CbHeading>The Thesis: The Moment of Maximum Leverage Is the Only Moment to Negotiate Ownership</CbHeading>

<CbDropCap>In 2016, Maggie Rogers played a song for Pharrell Williams during a masterclass at NYU. He stopped the room. The video went viral — millions of views. Every major label called. Most 22-year-olds in that position sign whatever is put in front of them. Rogers did not. She negotiated a deal with Capitol Records where she licenses her music to them through her own imprint, Debay Sounds. The distinction matters enormously: in a traditional record deal, the label owns your masters. In Rogers' structure, Debay Sounds owns the music and Capitol distributes it.</CbDropCap>

How did a 22-year-old walk into a boardroom of executives and negotiate that? Starting the summer after her freshman year at NYU, she had worked as Lizzy Goodman's intern and de facto assistant editor on *Meet Me in the Bathroom*, an oral history of the early-2000s New York music scene. Over three years she transcribed more than 200 hours of interviews with musicians who had been chewed up by the label system. She also walked in with a bound copy of her 20-page NYU senior thesis — a business plan for her own career. When the labels came calling, she showed up with stories from their own histories: "I would show up with ammo," she told the New York Times. "Really minute stories from their lives, like where they were on 9/11, or that time they tried to sign the Killers in 2007."

<CbPullQuote>Rogers did at 22 what most musicians never learn to do at all: she understood that the moment of maximum leverage — the viral moment, the bidding war — is the one moment where you can negotiate ownership. And she used it. One decision at the moment of maximum leverage will define the economics of her entire career.</CbPullQuote>

For the library, Rogers is the **licensing-over-assignment case** — proof that retaining ownership while accessing major label infrastructure is possible if you negotiate during the window of maximum leverage. She is also the **strategic pause case**: a year at Harvard Divinity School studying the spirituality of public gathering and the ethics of power in pop culture, which gave her a framework for the live experience that is now the most valuable part of her business. The two structures we read against Rogers's career — Exclusive Licensing and Premium Service — are our framework for the licensing-over-assignment arc. Rogers didn't pick those structures off a menu in 2016; she negotiated a Capitol licensing deal because she'd done the homework on what other artists wished they had asked for. The fit between what she did and what the structures describe is what makes the case useful.

</CbSection>

<CbSection id="pause">

<CbHeading>The Strategic Pause: Harvard Divinity School as R&D</CbHeading>

<CbTable
  headersJson='["What Most Artists Would Do", "What Rogers Did", "What It Actually Accomplished"]'
  rowsJson='[["Push through burnout", "Enrolled at Harvard Divinity School, September 2021", "Rest (necessary)"],["Quick follow-up album", "Studied the spirituality of public gathering", "Intellectual depth (differentiating)"],["Maximize Grammy momentum", "Wrote a thesis on the ethics of power in pop culture", "Album emerged from genuine inquiry (Surrender)"],["Stay visible at all costs", "Returned as a Religion and Public Life Fellow (2023-24)", "Framework for understanding her own work"],["Rush back to touring", "Took an academic year out at peak momentum", "One year off the release treadmill → career deepening"]]'
/>

<CbPullQuote>Fan reviews of the arena tour consistently use quasi-spiritual language: transcendent, liberation, out of body. Rogers did not stumble into this. She studied it, wrote a thesis about it, and designed performances around it. The Harvard detour was not a pause. It was R&D for the most valuable part of her business — the live experience.</CbPullQuote>

Two things keep this from being a tidier story than it is. The program was **one academic year**, not a sabbatical — she enrolled in September 2021 and graduated in May 2022, in the inaugural cohort of a degree Harvard built for mid-career professionals. And the payoff was not immediate: *Surrender* arrived two months after graduation, and the tour behind it played theaters and amphitheaters through 2023, including Radio City again. The arenas came in October 2024, off a different album written two years later. The framework compounded. It did not convert on contact.

Develop craft over years (two independent albums, NYU production degree). Viral moment creates maximum leverage (Pharrell video, bidding war). Negotiate ownership at that moment (Debay Sounds licensing deal). Strategic pause deepens the work (a year at Harvard Divinity; the thesis and the album share a title). Come back bigger (theaters → arenas, MSG). Masters compound over a career.

The hub is "Own Your Masters" because the entire flywheel depends on the licensing deal Rogers negotiated at 22. Four albums have come out through Debay Sounds — three studio records and a compilation — and each one is an asset she holds rather than one she will someday have to buy back. That is the difference the structure makes: not a reversion date to wait for, but a catalog that was never anyone else's. One decision at 22 changes the lifetime economics of the career.

<CbSection id="lessons">

<CbHeading>Transferable Lessons</CbHeading>

<CbAccordion>
  <CbAccordionCard num="01" label="The Moment of Maximum Leverage Is the Only Moment to Negotiate Ownership">
    <p className="cb-card-text">When every buyer is calling — the bidding war, the viral moment, the breakout — you have leverage you will never have again. Rogers used this moment to secure a licensing deal rather than a traditional recording contract. Most artists do not know this option exists. Most who know it lack the courage to ask. Preparation made the difference.</p>
  </CbAccordionCard>
  <CbAccordionCard num="02" label="Research Is the Best Negotiation Prep That Exists">
    <p className="cb-card-text">Three years and 200-plus hours transcribing Lizzy Goodman's oral history gave Rogers intimate knowledge of how the label system had mistreated previous generations. She walked into boardrooms knowing the executives' own histories, and she brought a written 20-page plan for what she wanted to build. This is not abstract preparation — it is competitive intelligence gathered through genuine intellectual curiosity, paired with a document that turned the meeting from an audition into a pitch. Study your industry's history and it gives you power in its present.</p>
  </CbAccordionCard>
  <CbAccordionCard num="03" label="Licensing Beats Assignment When You Have Leverage">
    <p className="cb-card-text">If someone wants to distribute your work, explore whether you can license it to them (retaining ownership) rather than assigning it (transferring ownership). The difference compounds over a career. Debay Sounds owns the masters; Capitol distributes. Rogers never has to run the buy-back campaign that has consumed years of other artists' careers, because the asset never left. Same principle applies beyond music: any creative licensing IP to a distributor should explore this structure.</p>
  </CbAccordionCard>
  <CbAccordionCard num="04" label="Strategic Pauses Are R&D, Not Retreat">
    <p className="cb-card-text">Rogers nearly quit music after three years of non-stop touring. Instead of grinding through burnout, she spent an academic year at Harvard Divinity School. That year gave her rest, intellectual depth, an album born from genuine inquiry, and a conceptual framework that now differentiates her live performances. Note the lag: she graduated in May 2022 and did not headline an arena until October 2024. A pause is an investment, and investments take time to price in.</p>
  </CbAccordionCard>
  <CbAccordionCard num="05" label="What Wouldn't Transfer">
    <p className="cb-card-text">**The Pharrell viral moment is non-replicable.** You cannot manufacture a video of the world's most famous producer being moved to silence by your song. **The Clive Davis Institute network.** NYU's program provides access most music students do not have — including the room Pharrell walked into. **The specific deal terms are unknown.** The licensing structure is confirmed and Rogers has said she owns her masters, but the economics, the term, and what happens at its end are not public. Do not assume "licensing deal" means the same thing in your industry that it meant in hers.</p>
    <p className="cb-card-text">**But the licensing-over-assignment principle transfers completely.** Know the deal structures before you need them. Negotiate ownership at the moment of maximum leverage. Create your own entity before signing anything. Release independently to prove you can. Take strategic pauses when needed.</p>
  </CbAccordionCard>
